Dear Stacy,

I’ve been thinking about our last conversation and everything that has happened between us. I’ve taken time to reflect on my actions, and I understand more clearly how much I hurt, disappointed, and embarrassed you.

For a long time, I focused on defending myself instead of stepping back and recognising my own mistakes. Looking back, I can see how my actions let you down, even when things between us seemed to be going well. I understand why this affected your trust in me.

Regarding my decision to talk to Sharon and Mary, even if I believed I was trying to save our relationship, I didn’t fully consider the consequences. I can understand how involving them may have exposed something personal between us and left you feeling disappointed and embarrassed. I’m genuinely sorry for putting you in that position.

I know an apology cannot undo what happened, and I don’t expect you to forget it simply because I’m sorry. I also understand that forgiveness doesn’t automatically make the hurt disappear or bring trust back overnight.

I regret the choices I made, but I can’t change the past. What I can do is take responsibility, learn from this, and work on changing the behaviours that caused you pain. I know that real change has to be shown through consistent actions, not just promises in a letter.

You still matter to me, Stacy, and I value what we have shared. I’m not writing this to pressure you, make you feel guilty, or ask you to pretend everything is fine. I simply want you to know that I recognise the impact of my actions and that my apology is sincere.

If we both decide that we want to work towards rebuilding what was damaged, I would welcome the chance to do that with more honesty, respect, and maturity. I understand that it has to be a choice we both make, and I will respect your feelings and the time you need.

I can’t promise I’ll never make a mistake again, but I can promise to take responsibility for my choices and make a genuine effort to do better. I don’t expect you to trust my words immediately; I hope, over time, my actions can speak for themselves.

Thank you for the time, care, memories, and dreams we have shared. I don’t take them for granted. Whatever you decide, I wanted to offer you an honest apology and acknowledge my part in what happened.

I’m truly sorry, Stacy. Please take your time to process this. You don’t have to reply immediately.

With sincerity,
John
